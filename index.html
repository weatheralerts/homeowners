Small jobs that prevent big repairs

Much of the weather damage to homes is preventable with a little notice. Clearing a drain before a heavy downpour, lagging an exposed pipe before a hard freeze, tying down the garden furniture before a gusty night or moving the car out from under a tree before a storm: each of these takes minutes, but only if you know the weather is coming.

Weather Alerts is an Android app that turns the forecast into personal notifications. You decide which conditions to watch and at what level, and the app notifies you when they are expected. The Weather Alerts website explains it simply: the app monitors the conditions you choose and notifies you when they reach your selected thresholds.

Household risks and the alerts that match

Downpours and gutters

Rain alerts with light, moderate and heavy thresholds tell you when a cloudburst is expected, a good moment to check gutters, gullies and basement drains.

Freezing nights and pipes

Cold and freezing-conditions alerts prompt you to protect outdoor taps, garden hoses and exposed pipework.

Gusts and loose items

Wind gust thresholds warn you before garden furniture, trampolines, bins and fence panels are put to the test.

Snow and freezing rain

Snow, sleet and freezing rain alerts give time to grit the drive and plan the morning.

Hail and thunderstorms

Weather description alerts can flag hail and thunderstorms, a cue to move the car or protect greenhouse panes.

Humidity and condensation

Humidity thresholds help you plan ventilation, laundry drying and dehumidifier use.

Set up a home weather watch

  1. Save your property. Add your home, and if you own or manage more, a rental flat, a holiday cottage or an elderly parent's house.
  2. Add a few house rules. Heavy rain, freezing conditions, gusts above a chosen speed and hail are a good start.
  3. Pick your notice period. Be told immediately or up to seven days before, enough to book a gutter clean or buy pipe insulation.

Each property keeps its own alerts, so a hillside cottage and a city flat can have very different thresholds.

Official warnings for your area

When the weather turns properly severe, your own rules sit alongside official severe weather warnings from supported national meteorological services, shown with agency attribution. Depending on where you live, these may include flash flood, severe thunderstorm, tornado, hurricane, winter storm and blizzard warnings.

Custom notification controls let critical warnings come through at any hour, while Quiet Hours and Active Hours keep routine alerts from waking the household. Pick a custom sound so you know a weather alert from a text message at a glance.

See what is coming

Live radar shows rain, snow, hail and storms moving across the map, the storm radar highlights developing severe weather, and the lightning tracker follows thunderstorm activity. Hourly forecasts show temperature, feels-like, precipitation probability, wind and humidity, and daily forecasts look several days ahead. A home-screen widget shows current conditions, official warnings and active custom alerts without opening the app.

A stormy weekend, handled

Thursday evening a gust alert for Saturday night arrives for your address. On Friday you stack the garden chairs in the shed, tie down the trampoline and move the bins behind the wall. Saturday afternoon a heavy rain rule fires as well, so you clear leaves from the gully by the back door. When the storm passes overnight, the only job left on Sunday is sweeping up a few twigs.

That is the whole idea: notice turns into a couple of small tasks, and small tasks prevent repairs. The same pattern works for freezing spells, snow and hail.

Renters benefit too

You do not need to own a house for this to help. Tenants can protect bikes, balcony plants and cars, and let a landlord know early if a storm or a freeze is forecast and something needs attention. Students in shared houses can use a freezing-conditions alert as a reminder to keep the heating ticking over during holidays.

Landlords and second-home owners

If you look after more than one property, saving each address with its own rules means a frost alert for an empty holiday home reaches you even when you are far away. That gives time to ask a neighbour to check the heating or to arrange a visit before a freeze, rather than finding a burst pipe weeks later.

Weather Alerts gives you forecast-based notifications; it does not connect to sensors, thermostats or alarms in the property.

Frequently asked questions

Can I get an alert before a heavy downpour hits my house?

Yes. Set a heavy rain threshold for your home location and you will be notified when it is forecast.

Will the app remind me to protect pipes in a freeze?

Set a freezing-conditions or low-temperature alert for your address and use the notification as your reminder.

Can I watch a rental flat and a holiday home as well as my own house?

Yes. Add each property and give it its own set of weather rules.

Does Weather Alerts connect to smart home devices?

No. It provides forecast-based alerts and does not link to sensors, thermostats or alarms.

Is there a hail warning feature?

Weather description alerts can flag hail and thunderstorms in the forecast.

Are flood warnings included?

Official flash flood warnings appear where supported national meteorological services issue them, depending on region.

Can I keep alerts quiet at night except for serious warnings?

Yes. Quiet Hours hold routine notifications back, and critical warnings are still allowed through.
